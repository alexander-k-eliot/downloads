The GEO Playbook for Financial Advisors

How to make your firm the answer AI assistants give when someone asks who to trust with their money

By Click Coded (Alexander K. Eliot). AI-operated, human-reviewed, disclosed everywhere.

This is the vertical edition of The GEO Playbook, rebuilt for RIAs and independent advisors. Same technical foundation, adapted for a field where content has to clear compliance review before it ships, and where the wrong kind of visibility guide would tell you to do something your CCO would flag.

Nobody can guarantee an AI assistant recommends your firm. This playbook does not promise that. It gives you the verifiable, technical conditions that make citation possible, so the content your compliance process approves has an actual chance of being read by the models your prospects are asking.

Scope, stated up front: this is a technical GEO/structured-data guide, not legal or compliance advice. It does not tell you how to satisfy the SEC Marketing Rule, FINRA Rule 2210, or your firm's own compliance manual. Where those rules shape a recommendation, this guide says so and stops there. Talk to your compliance officer about anything that touches disclosure, testimonials, or performance claims.

Module 1 — What we found when we actually checked

A quarter of consumers already say they plan to use ChatGPT, Gemini, or another AI tool to start their search for a financial advisor. Financial services also shows the highest measured SEO return on investment of any industry benchmarked in 2026, ahead of biotech and B2B SaaS. That's the demand-side case every GEO guide in this space already makes.

What we did differently: we ran our own AgentReady audit — the same 9-point machine-readability test behind our SaaS benchmark — against 16 real financial advisory and wealth-management firms, from national broker-dealers to modern RIAs (Edward Jones, Ameriprise, Charles Schwab, Vanguard, Betterment, Northwestern Mutual, and others). Full ranking and methodology: the financial services benchmark page.

Average score: 67.2/100. Median: 70/100. The spread was the real finding: three household names — Northwestern Mutual (15), Charles Schwab (25), and Vanguard (50) — scored among the worst in the sample, while newer, digital-native firms like Betterment and Hightower cleared 90. Brand recognition and AI-visibility are not the same thing, and several of the most established names in the industry are learning that the hard way. Only 44% of the firms we checked had published an llms.txt file, and just 13% had agents.md guidance — the two files AI agents actually read to understand what a firm does. eMarketer runs a standing AI Visibility Index for financial services and updates it quarterly; this is being tracked at the industry-analyst level, not just discussed on marketing blogs, and most firms still aren't showing up in the ways that index rewards.

The gap: almost none of the existing GEO content is built specifically for how advisor marketing actually works, inside a compliance review process, with FINRA BrokerCheck and SEC IAPD sitting alongside your own site as sources a model can check you against. Generic GEO advice assumes you can publish anything today. You can't, and that constraint should shape the plan, not get ignored by it.

Module 2 — The machine-readability foundation

Same four gates as any site, with one advisor-specific wrinkle: a large share of RIA websites run on compliance-oriented platform builders (Twenty Over Ten, FMG Suite, Snappy Kraken, and similar). These platforms exist to solve the compliance-review problem, but they often ship JavaScript-heavy templates that were never optimized for machine readability. Solving compliance and solving crawlability are two different problems, and most advisor sites have only solved the first one.

2.1 Server-rendered content

View source (not inspect element) on your homepage and your bio page. If your name, credentials, and firm description aren't sitting in the raw HTML, a non-JS-executing AI crawler sees an empty page, regardless of how the site looks in a browser.

2.2 robots.txt with an explicit AI policy

Confirm /robots.txt explicitly addresses the major AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Google-Extended). An empty or missing file isn't neutral, it's undecided, and undecided reads as unavailable to most of these systems. The free robots.txt generator builds this in under a minute.

2.3 llms.txt

A short markdown index at yoursite.com/llms.txt: firm name, what you do, who you serve, links to your ADV, your bio, and your contact page. The free generator fills this from a short form.

2.4 XML sitemap

Confirm /sitemap.xml exists and is referenced from robots.txt. Advisor sites frequently have thin sitemaps because most of the site is static compliance boilerplate, not content, which makes this an easy check to pass once and forget.

Module 3 — Structured data for advisor sites

3.1 Organization / FinancialService schema


{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FinancialService",
  "name": "Your Firm Name",
  "url": "https://yourfirm.com",
  "logo": "https://yourfirm.com/logo.png",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/firm/summary/YOUR-CRD",
    "https://www.linkedin.com/company/yourfirm"
  ]
}

The sameAs field matters more here than in most industries: pointing to your own SEC IAPD or FINRA BrokerCheck listing is a direct, verifiable trust signal a model can check, not a claim it has to take on faith.

3.2 Person schema for the advisor

Individual advisors are often the actual entity a prospect is asking about, not just the firm. A Person schema block on each advisor's bio page, with honorificSuffix for CFP/CFA/ChFC-style credentials and a sameAs link to their own BrokerCheck or IAPD individual record, gives a model the same kind of checkable signal the firm-level markup does.

3.3 FAQPage schema — the safest high-leverage content type you have

General financial education content — "What is a fee-only fiduciary," "How does a Roth conversion work," "What's the difference between a CFP and a broker" — is usually the content type advisors can publish with the lightest compliance lift, because it explains concepts rather than making claims about performance or suitability. It's also exactly the shape (a direct question, a direct answer) that AI systems disproportionately quote. The overlap between "easiest to get approved" and "most likely to get cited" is the single best-leveraged move in this guide. The free FAQ schema generator and FAQ content scanner handle the markup once you've written honest, real answers.

The rule that matters more than the code: only mark up questions your page visibly, actually answers. Mismatched schema is exactly what search and AI systems are built to detect, and for a regulated business the reputational cost of getting caught cutting that corner is much higher than the upside.

Module 4 — Writing content that clears compliance and gets quoted

4.1 Educational, not promotional

Content framed as "here's how X works" clears review faster than content framed as "here's why you should pick us," and it happens to be the framing AI systems trust and cite more readily too. This is one of the rare cases where the safest compliance posture and the most citable content strategy point the same direction.

4.2 Answer-first paragraphs

Put the direct answer in the first sentence under each heading. Save context, caveats, and required disclosures for after. A model quoting your page pulls the self-contained sentence, not the paragraph that gets to the point eventually.

4.3 Credentials and specialization, stated plainly

"CFP, specializing in retirement income planning for small business owners" is a concrete, quotable fact. "Comprehensive wealth management solutions" is not. Say exactly who you serve and what you're credentialed in, on the page, in plain language.

4.4 One page, one question

A page trying to cover retirement planning, tax strategy, and estate planning at once answers none of them cleanly enough to quote. Split broad topics into focused pages built around single questions.

Module 5 — Identity consistency across the sources a model actually checks

This is the module most specific to this industry. A model forming an opinion about an advisor has more checkable, structured, third-party sources available than in almost any other vertical:

  • FINRA BrokerCheck and SEC IAPD: your registered name, firm, and disciplinary history, if any, live here independent of anything you publish. Confirm your name and firm name match your website exactly.
  • Form ADV Part 2 (your firm's own disclosure brochure): often the most detailed, plain-English description of what your firm actually does that exists anywhere, and it's already public. Link to it. Don't make a model choose between trusting your marketing copy and trusting your own regulatory filing when they can agree.
  • Your website, LinkedIn, and Google Business Profile: same name, same credentials, same firm description, everywhere. Fragmented identity reads as a weaker, less-trusted entity to both search engines and AI systems.

Module 6 — Measuring and iterating

1. Run the free checker on your homepage and your top advisor bio page today. Save the score.

2. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity directly what they know about your firm and its advisors. Save the actual answers.

3. Fix the highest-weight failing checks first: machine-readability and structured data outweigh polish.

4. Re-run the checker and re-ask the assistants in four to six weeks.

Module 7 — Your 30-day plan

Week 1: Module 2 (robots.txt, llms.txt, sitemap, server-rendering check). These gate everything else and don't touch compliance review at all.

Week 2: Module 3 (FinancialService + Person schema) on your homepage and every advisor bio page.

Week 3: Draft 3-5 educational FAQ entries with your compliance team, using Module 4's framing, and mark them up with Module 3.3's schema once approved.

Week 4: Module 5 (identity sweep across BrokerCheck, IAPD, ADV Part 2, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile) + Module 6 (baseline measurement, save your results).

Repeat the measurement step every 4-6 weeks after that.

Honest scope

This playbook teaches the real, verifiable technical conditions that make AI citation possible for a financial advisory practice. It does not, and cannot, guarantee that any specific assistant cites, ranks, or recommends your firm, and it is not legal or compliance advice. Nothing here tells you how to satisfy the SEC Marketing Rule or FINRA Rule 2210. That determination belongs to your compliance officer, on your specific content, every time.

What you can control: whether the technical conditions exist, and whether the content you already have compliance approval to publish is actually readable by the systems your prospects are asking. This playbook is how you build that yourself.
